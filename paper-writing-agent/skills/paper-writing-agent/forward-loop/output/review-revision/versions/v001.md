# Sleep Regularity and Cardiovascular Events in a Community Cohort

This is a synthetic manuscript for testing a writing workflow. All participants, results and identifiers below are fictional.

## Abstract

Sleep regularity may be related to cardiovascular health. In this prospective observational cohort, we observed baseline sleep regularity in 480 adults and followed them for five years. There were 8 cardiovascular events among 160 participants with higher regularity and 24 events among 320 participants with lower regularity. The adjusted hazard ratio was 0.68 (95% CI 0.31–1.50; P=0.34). The point estimate suggested a lower hazard with higher sleep regularity, but the association was uncertain. These observational findings do not establish a preventive effect of sleep regularity.

## Methods

This prospective observational cohort enrolled 480 adults without a history of cardiovascular disease. Sleep regularity was measured at baseline and was not assigned by the investigators. Follow-up lasted five years. We estimated a Cox proportional hazards model adjusted for baseline age and sex. The analysis used complete observations; no included participant had a missing value for these variables. The synthetic study protocol identifier is SYN-001. No external validation study was performed.

## Results

There were 160 participants in the higher-regularity group and 320 in the lower-regularity group. We observed 8 and 24 events, respectively. The adjusted hazard ratio comparing higher with lower regularity was 0.68 (95% CI 0.31–1.50; P=0.34).

## Discussion

The point estimate was compatible with lower risk, but uncertainty was substantial. Sleep regularity was observed rather than assigned, so residual confounding remains possible. The study did not test a sleep intervention. Additional studies would be needed to clarify the association.

## Conclusion

In this prospective observational cohort, the point estimate suggested a lower hazard of cardiovascular events with higher sleep regularity, but the association remained uncertain. The study did not establish a preventive effect.
